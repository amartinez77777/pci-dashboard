# PCI Evidence Limitations | Stage 05

**Book occurrence vs scientific validity.** The presence of a sentence in a 2002 revised self-help text only establishes its attribution to that edition. Claims in canonical records can carry `verified_in_pdf` even when `evidence_status` is `book_assertion_only`, `not_assessed` or when external evidence addresses only narrow portions.

**Goal-seeking cybernetics.** The automatic success/failure mechanism and internal servo-mechanism are explanatory comparisons inspired by control and feedback systems. It is not a measured standalone brain circuit; the author's claims that it controls all behaviors or assures success should be presented as book claims, not computational neuroscience.

**Imagination and rehearsal.** Sport mental imagery literature includes task-specific results and mixed mechanisms. Imagery can be an optional adjunct to preparation, but it is not equivalent to deliberate physical practice, risk-free for everyone, or proof that an imagined action guarantees an outcome. The inherited systematic review DOI 10.1080/17408989.2021.1991297 and motor imagery review PMCID PMC11315805 were bibliographically located; their full critical risk-of-bias assessment remains open.

**Hypnosis and belief.** NCCIH's hypnosis factsheet reports condition-specific studies with evidence limitations; it does not support global clinical claims. Do not equate restricting beliefs with diagnosed hypnosis or substitute practice activities for appropriate healthcare. Avoid labeling users with psychological diagnoses.

**Extraordinary or paranormal assertions.** The revised book reports telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition and a universal storehouse of information as established. The identified 2026 neuroimaging review surveys disputed, heterogeneous literature and does not prove such abilities. Present the extraordinary proposition as attributed and unresolved/unsupported, not a verified phenomenon.

**Aging, illness and longevity.** Chapter 15 explicitly introduces speculative over-beliefs and broad health interpretations; no reader should infer that changing self-image can treat illness, reverse aging, restore muscles or guarantee longevity. Stage 05 did not clinically validate these statements. Keep activity design behavioral/reflective and stop before any treatment recommendation.

**Case histories.** The author's plastic-surgery anecdotes and Chapter 16 testimonials are evidence of what is narrated, not controlled outcomes. Plausible alternative explanations include selection, recall and publication bias, social support, natural change and undisclosed concurrent interventions.

**Practices and editorial voice.** The 2002 edition is a composite revision. Timing/frequency are chapter specific, and source attribution between Maltz and Kennedy may be unclear. Do not impose a universal 21-day schedule, require forgiveness or disclosure of emotionally painful history, or infer invisible subconscious changes from exercise logs.

**Scope of this audit.** 16 PDF-page spot checks plus 4 limited external bibliographic checks. Not an exhaustive independent evidence systematic review. 55 tracked issues are supplied; no claims of complete scientific verification.
